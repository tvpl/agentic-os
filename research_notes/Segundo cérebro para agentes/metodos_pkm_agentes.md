# PKM methods and AI second brains for agents (2024-2026), compared with MordomoOS

Note on dates: method sources are older primary texts (2017-2023, flagged). Agent-practice sources are 2025-2026. Several practitioner sources are newsletters or vendor blogs (MindStudio, Substack) and were not independently verified. Those are flagged "practitioner/secondary".

## 1. Method essentials and how they translate to agent-maintained memory

### Takeaway

The methods share four steps. Capture is cheap and temporary. Organization follows actionability (PARA) or links (Zettelkasten/MOC) rather than a deep taxonomy. Distillation is done in your own words, a little at a time, when you next touch a note. Maintenance is a fixed ritual (GTD weekly review). An agent can take on the bookkeeping parts: IDs, indexes, links, inbox triage, rollups. The methods' authors are explicit that the thinking and own-words writing are the point, so an agent should not take those over.

### Cited Findings

**Zettelkasten (zettelkasten.de, Sascha, 2020-10-27)**

- Atomicity: "one knowledge building block per note". The article calls it a direction for processing, not a strict rule. — [zettelkasten.de intro](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/)
- Own words: "use your own words, instead of just copying and pasting something you believe is useful". Verbatim quotes may be added but cannot replace this. — [zettelkasten.de intro](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/)
- "The unique identifier (ID) is mandatory to create a Zettelkasten." Accepted ID styles are Luhmann-style, time-based, arbitrary string and title-based. — [zettelkasten.de intro](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/)
- "A Zettelkasten makes connecting and not collecting a priority." Each link should carry a stated reason (the "link context"). Links without an explanation do not create knowledge. — [zettelkasten.de intro](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/)
- Structure notes are meta-notes about other notes and how they relate. Hub notes list entry points into a topic. — [zettelkasten.de intro](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/)
- Note lifecycle (Ahrens, _How to Take Smart Notes_, 2017; secondary summaries):
  - Fleeting notes are memory aids. They are written on the spot, rewritten into permanent notes if useful, and discarded within a day or two.
  - Literature notes are short, in your own words, and carry the bibliographic reference.
  - Permanent notes come from a daily review of fleeting and literature notes. Each holds one idea, is understandable in isolation, is compared with existing notes and linked to them.
  - Sources disagree on exactly when fleeting notes are discarded.
  - [Zettelkasten forum](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/14175/); [ybrikman summary](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2023/08/10/how-to-take-smart-notes/); [greaterwrong summary](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/T382CLwAjsy3fmecf/how-to-take-smart-notes-ahrens-2017)

**Evergreen notes (Andy Matuschak, undated working notes)**

- The principles are listed as note titles:
  - "Evergreen notes should be atomic"
  - "…should be concept-oriented"
  - "…should be densely linked"
  - "Prefer associative ontologies to hierarchical taxonomies"
  - "Write notes for yourself by default, disregarding audience"
  - [Evergreen notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Evergreen_notes)
- "'Better note-taking' misses the point; what matters is 'better thinking'". Also: "Most people take only transient notes." — [Evergreen notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Evergreen_notes)
- Supporting practices: a reading inbox for possibly useful references, and a writing inbox for transient and incomplete notes. — [Evergreen notes](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Evergreen_notes)

**PARA / CODE / Progressive Summarization (Tiago Forte)**

- PARA:
  - Projects are short-term efforts toward a specific goal.
  - Areas are ongoing responsibilities with no endpoint, such as Health or Finances.
  - Resources are topics of interest.
  - Archives hold inactive items from the other three.
  - Information is organized by actionability, around current commitments, not academic subjects.
  - Published 2023-02-24, updated 2026-04-15. — [Forte, PARA](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/)
- CODE stands for Capture, Organize, Distill, Express: "a simple four-step method… not only to build your Second Brain but also to work with it going forward". — [Forte, BASB overview](https://fortelabs.com/blog/basboverview/)
  - Secondary glosses: "Capture: keep what resonates; Organize: save for actionability; Distill: find the essence; Express: show your work". Express is called the most important step. I did not verify that wording in the book. — [jiffynotes](https://jiffynotes.substack.com/p/deep-dive-code-your-notes-so-you); [mmapped summary](https://mmapped.blog/posts/16-building-a-second-brain)
- Progressive Summarization (2017-12-27, updated 2023-05-16) has five layers:
  - L0: the original source.
  - L1: the captured note.
  - L2: bold the key passages.
  - L3: highlight the best of the bold.
  - L4: an executive summary in your own words, for only a few notes.
  - Remix: turn a tiny minority into something new.
  - [Forte, Progressive Summarization](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization-a-practical-technique-for-designing-discoverable-notes/)
- Summarize opportunistically: only when you are already engaging with a note, in small amounts, and only as much as the material deserves. — [Forte, Progressive Summarization](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization-a-practical-technique-for-designing-discoverable-notes/)
- Compression helps discovery and context helps understanding: "you cannot compress something without losing some of its context". Earlier layers are kept so that cuts are reversible. — [Forte, Progressive Summarization](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization-a-practical-technique-for-designing-discoverable-notes/)

**Maps of Content / LYT (Nick Milo)**

- An MOC is a note made mostly of links to other notes, a "map" of part of the library. — [LYT MOCs overview](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/mocs-overview); [LYT glossary](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/lyt-glossary)
- You create an MOC when you hit a "Mental Squeeze Point", the moment "your unsorted knowledge becomes so messy it overwhelms and discourages you". Creation follows need; it is not decided up front. — [LYT, Benefits of LYT frameworks](https://www.linkingyourthinking.com/ideaverse/benefits-of-lyt-frameworks); [LYT glossary](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/lyt-glossary)
- MOCs go through phases: gather, then collide, then navigate. — [LYT, The 3 phases of MOCs](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/the-3-phases-of-mocs)

**Johnny.Decimal (johnnydecimal.com, undated)**

- Structure:
  - Up to 10 areas, numbered in blocks such as 10-19.
  - Up to 10 categories per area.
  - IDs of the form `AC.ID`, such as `15.22`.
  - Up to 100 IDs per category.
  - Numbers are stable and don't shift when new items are added.
  - "create an index to link everything together" (the JDex).
  - [Johnny.Decimal intro](https://johnnydecimal.com/10-19-concepts/11-core/11.01-introduction/)
- The page is inconsistent about whether IDs start at 01 or .11. — [Johnny.Decimal intro](https://johnnydecimal.com/10-19-concepts/11-core/11.01-introduction/)

**GTD (David Allen)**

- The weekly review has three phases:
  - Get Clear: collect loose items, process inboxes to zero, empty your head.
  - Get Current: review next actions, past and upcoming calendar, waiting-for list, projects (each needs at least one next action) and checklists.
  - Get Creative: review someday/maybe and capture new ideas.
  - Allen calls the weekly review the "critical success factor".
  - [GTD podcast ep. 43](https://gettingthingsdone.com/2018/08/episode-43-the-power-of-the-gtd-weekly-review/); [noisydeadlines notes on ch. 8](https://tiny.write.as/noisydeadlines/gtd-notes-chapter-08-the-weekly-review)
- Doist recommends Friday afternoon and at least one hour. — [Doist, weekly review](https://doist.com/blog/weekly-review)

### Inferences

- How each method maps to agent memory:
  - Zettelkasten's ID and link-context rules call for stable IDs or slugs on every memory entry, with links that carry a reason, for example "supersedes X because…".
  - Atomicity calls for one fact or decision per entry. Claude Code's auto memory already does this: one memory per file, plus a one-line-per-entry index (section 3).
- The fleeting, literature, permanent lifecycle lines up with MordomoOS's daily journal, then nightly consolidation, then MEMORY.md:
  - Journal entries are the fleeting notes.
  - MEMORY.md Facts and Decisions are the permanent notes.
  - The gap: the methods require a person to rewrite the idea in their own words. MordomoOS consolidation is automatic, so MEMORY.md is an agent-written layer. It should be labeled that way and kept separate from user-authored notes.
- PARA versus MordomoOS:
  - MordomoOS "areas" map to PARA Areas.
  - There is no explicit Projects layer (time-bound, with an end state and archiving on completion) and no Archive policy beyond "Superseded".
  - PARA treats moving items as they become active or inactive as routine. MordomoOS has no equivalent.
- MOC versus ROUTER.md:
  - ROUTER.md and areas/<area>.md work like MOCs or hub notes.
  - LYT says to create MOCs on demand at a squeeze point. MordomoOS generates them from configured areas.
  - A possible requirement: the hygiene report suggests a new area or MOC when one area file or topic passes a size or entry threshold.
- Progressive summarization means no lossy compression without a pointer back to the source. Each MEMORY.md entry should link to the journal day or source file it came from (L0 or L1).
- GTD's weekly review has no counterpart in MordomoOS. Nightly consolidation is daily-only, and there is no weekly "get current" pass over open loops ("each project has a next action"). It is the clearest gap among the rituals.
- Johnny.Decimal's stable numeric IDs and single index (JDex) resemble ROUTER.md. MordomoOS has no stable IDs for memory entries, which makes supersession and links fragile.

### Gaps

- I did not fetch Forte's primary page on PARA review cadence or moving items between categories; the PARA post says nothing on it.
- The exact CODE book wording was not verified; the glosses are secondary.
- I did not find a primary source for "daily notes" as a method (Obsidian/Roam convention); it is supported only by practitioner guides in section 3.
- Ahrens' lifecycle comes from secondary summaries, not the book.

## 2. What agents do well and badly when maintaining a vault

### Takeaway

Practitioners report that agents do well at mechanical bookkeeping: cross-references, renaming and relinking, metadata, indexes, lint for orphans and contradictions, and weekly rollups. The strongest objections come from Zettelkasten practitioners. In their view, letting an LLM write the notes or find the connections defeats the purpose, which is the thinking done while writing; one calls it a new form of the "collector's fallacy". Separately, there are documented security risks when agents both write and read persistent memory (memory poisoning).

### Cited Findings

- Karpathy's "LLM Wiki" (gist dated 2026-04-04):
  - People abandon wikis because "the maintenance burden grows faster than the value".
  - LLMs don't tire of updating cross-references, so upkeep costs stay near zero.
  - The human curates sources, asks the questions and decides what things mean. The LLM summarizes, cross-references and files.
  - [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)
- Karpathy's operations:
  - Ingest: one source may touch 10-15 pages. He prefers one source at a time, under close supervision.
  - Query: good answers are filed back into the wiki.
  - Lint: checks for contradictions, stale claims, orphan pages, missing concept pages and missing cross-references.
  - [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)
- The gist went viral: 16M+ views reported, and the gist reportedly gained more than 5,000 stars within days. Implicator says the pattern "isn't the code". — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/data/karpathy-shares-llm-knowledge-base-architecture-that-bypasses-rag-with-an); [Implicator](https://implicator.ai/karpathys-llm-wiki-got-16-million-views-the-code-isnt-the-point-2/)
- Stefan Imhoff (2026-03-07, updated 2026-03-25):
  - Claude Code did these tasks well: pulling books, people and media mentioned in daily notes into resource notes with metadata; backlinking them; converting and renaming images and updating links; generating graphs.
  - He used `/init` to generate CLAUDE.md for the vault, and Claude kept its own memory file about the vault.
  - The post reports no failures and gives no warnings about voice.
  - [Imhoff](https://stefanimhoff.de/agentic-note-taking-obsidian-claude-code)
- Zettelkasten-forum views:
  - AI-written notes are "a new form of the collector's fallacy", shallow processing that only feels productive.
  - Another user rejects "querying a question and pasting the answer into a new note" and "relying solely on LLMs to find connections", while accepting LLMs for learning and checking definitions.
  - Another calls accepting ChatGPT's offer to write notes "entirely contrary to the whole purpose of building a ZK".
  - Some accept AI for navigation and link suggestions, provided the user stays in control.
  - [ZK forum 25608](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/25608/); [ZK forum 20029](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/20029/); [ZK forum 23444](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/23444/); [ZK forum 25085](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/25085/)
- The Obsidian forum has the same argument: "writing is thinking", so write the notes yourself. — [Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/select-tools-for-zettelkasten-but-how/66067)
- Voice loss is concrete enough that tools now ship to fix it:
  - Claudesidian includes a `de-ai-ify` skill. — [claudesidian](https://github.com/heyitsnoah/claudesidian)
  - The Obsidian plugin "slop-chop" strips AI writing patterns, with keep/prefer/avoid word settings and a "slop score". Adoption is small: 32 downloads per the community page, 151 per obsidianstats. — [slop-chop](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/slop-chop); [obsidianstats](https://www.obsidianstats.com/plugins/slop-chop)
- Claudesidian's core principle is "AI amplifies thinking, not just writing". It separates "thinking mode" (questions, search, connections) from "writing mode", suggests opening with "I'm in thinking mode, not writing mode", and advises reading AI output before trusting it. — [claudesidian](https://github.com/heyitsnoah/claudesidian)
- Vault pollution from over-ingestion (practitioner/secondary): one builder found that dumping every source into the vault gave Claude "almost nothing useful", because there was no routing layer telling it what to look for and when. — [Sam Thomas Davies, 2026-04-06 (paywalled; via search snippet)](https://samuelthomasdavies.substack.com/p/claude-second-brain-setup)
- Conflicting instructions: a reader of Carl Vellotti's post warns that global, project and auto-memory instructions can conflict and "break things in weird ways". — [Aman Khan / Carl Vellotti, 2026-02-10](https://amankhan1.substack.com/p/how-carl-set-up-his-personal-os-in)
- Anthropic's docs agree: "if two instructions contradict each other, Claude may pick one arbitrarily". They recommend periodic review and a prompt audit (`/doctor prompt-audit`). — [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
- Context size (Chroma "context rot", 2025):
  - 18 frontier models degrade as input length grows, even on simple tasks.
  - Distractors close to the target topic hurt most.
  - This argues for small, routed memory over loading everything.
  - Figures come from a secondary summary. — [Chroma research](https://trychroma.com/research/context-rot); [W&B summary](https://wandb.ai/byyoung3/ml-news/reports/Chroma-Research-Warns-of-Context-Rot-as-LLMs-Falter-with-Long-Inputs--VmlldzoxMzczMjQ4MQ)
- Memory poisoning (2025-2026 preprints; search snippets only):
  - In a study of Claude Code and Codex, it was hard to make an agent overwrite its memory files from untrusted content. Payloads already planted in memory files did attack current and future sessions. — [arXiv 2609.13889](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13889)
  - Agents that write and retrieve memory more aggressively are more exploitable, and existing prompt-injection defenses cover this only partly. — [arXiv 2606.04329](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04329)
  - "Sleeper" fabricated memories can re-emerge across later conversations. — [arXiv 2605.15338](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.15338)
  - Poisoned procedures stored with past experiences cause lasting behavior drift. — [MemoryGraft](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.16962)
  - OWASP's agentic list names persistent memory poisoning as ASI06 (via the same search summary).

### Inferences

- Rule of thumb for the product: the agent owns structure (indexes, links, IDs, dedup suggestions, staleness flags, rollups) and the user owns meaning (Reflections, insights, final decisions). MordomoOS's MEMORY.md has a "Reflections" section filled by nightly consolidation. Agent-written reflections are exactly the content that ZK practitioners and the voice-loss tools object to. Requirement: mark Reflections as user-authored, or as an agent proposal that needs confirmation.
- Provenance and authorship markers (agent versus user, plus source pointer) per entry cover two problems at once: voice loss and memory poisoning. Claude Code already adds a `modified` timestamp to memory frontmatter (section 3).
- Karpathy's supervised, one-at-a-time ingest suggests consolidation should produce a diff or proposal the user can review, rather than silently rewriting MEMORY.md each night.
- Auto-linking without a stated reason breaks the Zettelkasten "link context" rule. Agent-suggested links should carry a one-line reason.

### Gaps

- I found no quantitative study (survey or measurement) of vault pollution or voice loss in AI-maintained vaults. The evidence is forum opinion and tooling.
- I did not find a write-up that documents a failure of Claude Code or Obsidian automation in detail. Practitioner posts read as success stories.
- The memory-poisoning findings come from search snippets of preprints, not full papers.

## 3. The 2025-2026 practice: AI second brains as local markdown folders for Claude Code, Codex and Cursor

### Takeaway

The common pattern:

- A plain markdown folder or Obsidian vault.
- A short root CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md that acts as a map ("pointers, not content"; under 100-300 lines).
- Nested or on-demand files: subdirectory CLAUDE.md, path rules, skills, index files.
- A daily note or `/today` routine and a weekly review command.
- Skills or slash commands that read vault files.
- git for history.
- Onboarding through an interview or setup wizard (`/init-bootstrap`, `/onboard`), not a raw brain dump.
- "Challenge me" behavior through optional coach output styles.

Team sharing is mostly done through git and repo-level files. Hosted Claude memory is per project, with admin controls.

### Cited Findings

**Platform facts (primary)**

- Claude Code memory types:
  - CLAUDE.md is user-written instructions, loaded every session.
  - Auto memory is Claude-written. The first 200 lines or 25KB of `MEMORY.md` load every session.
  - [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
- CLAUDE.md guidance:
  - Target under 200 lines per file; longer files cost more context and reduce adherence.
  - Multi-step procedures belong in skills, and part-specific rules in path-scoped `.claude/rules/`.
  - `@path` imports go up to 4 hops deep but don't reduce context cost.
  - [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
- Nested files: CLAUDE.md files above the working directory load at launch. Subdirectory CLAUDE.md files load on demand when Claude reads or edits a file in that subdirectory. Path-scoped rules load when Claude touches a matching file. — [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
- AGENTS.md support:
  - Claude Code reads AGENTS.md only when no CLAUDE.md or CLAUDE.local.md exists at or above the working directory, unless the setting `claude-md-and-agents-md` is on. This needs v2.1.277+.
  - Adding a CLAUDE.local.md silently stops AGENTS.md from loading.
  - [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
- Auto memory format:
  - Notes are typed `user` / `feedback` / `project` / `reference`.
  - Claude skips anything it can derive from code or git, and anything CLAUDE.md already says.
  - Layout: `MEMORY.md` is an index with one line per memory, plus one topic file per memory, read on demand.
  - Near the size limit, Claude Code tells Claude to "keep one line per entry, move detail into topic files, and merge or drop stale entries".
  - A `modified` ISO timestamp is added to frontmatter.
  - Memory is machine-local and not shared across machines.
  - [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
- Enforcement: CLAUDE.md is context, not configuration. Use PreToolUse hooks or managed settings such as `permissions.deny` to block actions. — [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
- AGENTS.md spec:
  - "A README for agents" with no required fields.
  - Nested files are allowed; "the closest one to the edited file takes precedence", and the chat prompt overrides all.
  - The main OpenAI repo had 88 AGENTS.md files.
  - Used by 60,000+ open-source projects.
  - Stewarded by the Agentic AI Foundation under the Linux Foundation.
  - Supported by Codex, Cursor, Jules, Aider, goose, Zed, Warp, VS Code, Devin, Copilot coding agent, Windsurf, Gemini CLI and others.
  - [agents.md](https://agents.md/)
- Claude app memory:
  - Each project has its own separate memory.
  - Released to Team and Enterprise in September 2025 and to Pro and Max on 2025-10-23.
  - Incognito chats don't save to memory.
  - Enterprise admins can disable memory for the organization.
  - [Anthropic, Memory](https://www.anthropic.com/news/memory); [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/anthropic-adds-memory-to-claude-team-and-enterprise-incognito-for-all)
- Obsidian's CEO kepano publishes agent skills (obsidian-markdown, obsidian-bases, json-canvas, obsidian-cli, defuddle) for Claude Code, Codex and OpenCode, following the Agent Skills spec at agentskills.io. — [kepano/obsidian-skills](https://github.com/kepano/obsidian-skills)

  - CLAUDE.md covers role and goals, the vault map and naming conventions, frontmatter format, start and end session routines (read today's daily note and the inbox at start, write a session summary at end), and output preferences.
  - Keep it to 200-300 lines.
  - Separate memory by how often it changes: working notes each session, project context weekly, identity profile rarely.
  - [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/build-ai-second-brain-claude-code-obsidian); [Pillitteri](https://pasqualepillitteri.it/en/news/962/obsidian-claude-code-second-brain-persistent-memory)

### Inferences

Gaps in MordomoOS against this practice:

1. **No onboarding interview or brain dump.** Peer kits all ship an interview (`/init-bootstrap`, `/onboard`) that generates identity, goals and areas. MordomoOS starts empty. Requirement: a guided onboarding that captures identity, a goals file (GOALS.md equivalent), areas, active projects and existing decisions, plus a brain-dump inbox that is triaged afterwards rather than written into AGENTS.md.
2. **No goals or "business-first" anchor.** Vellotti's GOALS.md and ballred's goal cascade and goal-aligner give the agent something to judge requests against. The "challenge me" behavior is an opt-in output style, not a default. Requirement: a goals/priorities section in the compiled AGENTS.md and an optional "push back when a request conflicts with goals" instruction. I found no primary source for a named "business-first / don't just agree" convention, so it should be presented as a product choice, not a standard.
3. **ROUTER.md plus areas/<area>.md compiled into AGENTS.md matches best practice** ("pointers, not content"; index first, as in Karpathy's index.md). Two risks:
   - Compiling area content into the root file inflates it past the 100-200 line guidance. The router should contain pointers, with area files loaded on demand.
   - Claude Code skips AGENTS.md whenever a CLAUDE.md or CLAUDE.local.md exists, so the sync command must write both files, or write CLAUDE.md with `@AGENTS.md`.
4. **No weekly or monthly cadence.** Peers ship a daily routine, a weekly review (Sunday) and a monthly rollup. MordomoOS has a daily journal and nightly consolidation only.
5. **No Projects or Archive lifecycle and no inbox folder.** Peers use PARA folders. MordomoOS has areas but no time-bound projects that get archived.
6. **No git or auto-commit for memory.** Peers commit each session or each write, which gives undo and audit for agent edits. Unless MordomoOS keeps history elsewhere, nightly rewrites of MEMORY.md cannot be undone.
7. **Single owner only.** The practice shares through git (AGENTS.md is committed and team-shared; CLAUDE.local.md is personal). Claude app memory is per project with admin disable. A team version would need shared versus private layers.

Other notes:

- MordomoOS MEMORY.md versus Claude auto memory: Claude Code caps its index at 200 lines or 25KB and pushes "one line per entry, detail in topic files". MordomoOS should enforce a similar budget and split, or MEMORY.md will grow without limit.

### Gaps

- I could not read the paywalled Sam Thomas Davies article or Teresa Torres' exact file structure.
- I found no credible write-up on access control (per-area permissions, redaction) for shared AI second brains. Team sharing in practice is just git.
- I did not survey YouTube videos directly.
- I did not verify Cursor-specific memory (`.cursor/rules`) in this pass.

## 4. Maintenance rituals: weekly review, staleness, duplicate merges, archive policy, link gardening

### Takeaway

The cadences that recur:

- Per session: start and end routine, commit.
- Daily: daily note, `/today` or standup, end-of-day review.
- Weekly: a GTD three-phase review or a "weekly synthesis" of the daily notes, open loops and stale projects.
- Monthly or quarterly: goal rollups and goals-file updates.
- Periodic lint: contradictions, stale claims, orphans, missing pages, broken links, duplicates.

I found no practitioner metrics (counts, thresholds) beyond the platform's own size limits.

### Cited Findings

- GTD: the weekly review is the "critical success factor": process inboxes to zero, every project has a next action, review someday/maybe. — [GTD ep. 43](https://gettingthingsdone.com/2018/08/episode-43-the-power-of-the-gtd-weekly-review/); [noisydeadlines](https://tiny.write.as/noisydeadlines/gtd-notes-chapter-08-the-weekly-review)
- Karpathy lint checks for contradictions, stale claims, orphan pages, missing concept pages, missing cross-references and data gaps a web search could fill. Each lint pass is logged in `log.md`. — [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)
- ballred: note-organizer fixes broken links and consolidates duplicates; `/weekly` runs Sunday; `/monthly` rolls up the weekly reviews and checks quarterly milestones. — [obsidian-claude-pkm](https://github.com/ballred/obsidian-claude-pkm)
- Claudesidian: daily-review (end of day) and weekly-synthesis ("patterns across the week"). — [claudesidian](https://github.com/heyitsnoah/claudesidian)
- MindStudio's weekly prompt summarizes the past week's daily notes, lists open loops and flags stale projects. — [MindStudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/build-ai-second-brain-claude-code-obsidian)
- Anthropic tells users to review CLAUDE.md files, nested files and rules periodically for outdated or conflicting instructions, and offers `/doctor prompt-audit`. Auto memory reminds Claude to "merge or drop stale entries" when MEMORY.md nears the 200-line or 25KB limit. — [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
- Vellotti updates CLAUDE.md only when the system changes, and GOALS.md quarterly. — [Aman Khan / Carl Vellotti](https://amankhan1.substack.com/p/how-carl-set-up-his-personal-os-in)
- Archive policy:
  - PARA: Archives hold inactive items from Projects, Areas and Resources. — [Forte, PARA](https://fortelabs.com/blog/para/)
  - Ahrens: fleeting notes are discarded within a day or two. — [ZK forum](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/14175/)
- LYT: create a new MOC when a topic hits a mental squeeze point (on demand, not scheduled). — [LYT glossary](https://blog.linkingyourthinking.com/notes/lyt-glossary)

### Inferences

Proposed requirements for MordomoOS:

- A weekly review routine in GTD form:
  - Get Clear: process the journal's Open loops to zero, meaning each is closed, turned into a project or task, or moved to someday.
  - Get Current: each area and project has a next action; flag stale ones.
  - Get Creative: someday/maybe.
- Expand the hygiene report into Karpathy-style lint:
  - contradictions between Facts and Decisions;
  - stale entries, with the `modified` timestamp and an age threshold;
  - orphan memory files not referenced from ROUTER.md;
  - duplicate entries (suggest a merge, don't merge silently);
  - broken links;
  - open loops older than N days;
  - an area file over its size budget (squeeze point, suggest a split).
- Metrics to report, which I propose because no source gives them:
  - number of open loops and their age distribution;
  - MEMORY.md lines and KB against the budget;
  - orphan count;
  - entries without provenance;
  - entries superseded this week.
- Archive policy: MordomoOS's "Superseded" section is a start, but it is not PARA's archive of completed projects. Requirement: move closed open loops and finished projects to an archive file or folder on a weekly cadence, keeping links to the source.

### Gaps

- I found no published thresholds from practitioners (how many days counts as stale, maximum open loops). Platform limits (200 lines, 25KB, under 200-line CLAUDE.md) are the only hard numbers.
- I found no evidence on how consistently practitioners keep up weekly reviews with agents over time.

## 5. Capture: what is worth capturing, meeting notes, provenance, privacy

### Takeaway

Across methods and platforms, the advice is the same:

- Capture is cheap and temporary (fleeting notes, an inbox, `_temp/`, brain dumps into a backlog).
- What gets promoted to durable memory is selective: decisions, preferences, corrections, non-derivable project context, and insights in your own words.
- Raw sources stay immutable and separate. Distilled entries point back to them.
- Privacy controls in practice are incognito modes, per-project scoping, machine-local storage and admin disable.

### Cited Findings

- Claude auto memory saves user role and preferences, feedback (corrections and confirmed approaches), project info Claude "can't derive from the code or git history" (ongoing work, deadlines, decisions), and references (where external info lives). It skips anything derivable or already in CLAUDE.md, and it "doesn't save something every session". — [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
- Capture selectively: "keep what resonates" (secondary gloss of CODE). L1 capture has no fixed selection criteria; distillation happens later and opportunistically. — [jiffynotes](https://jiffynotes.substack.com/p/deep-dive-code-your-notes-so-you); [Forte, Progressive Summarization](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization-a-practical-technique-for-designing-discoverable-notes/)
- Zettelkasten says "connecting and not collecting". Ahrens has literature notes keep the bibliographic reference, so sources can be traced. — [zettelkasten.de intro](https://zettelkasten.de/introduction/); [ybrikman](https://www.ybrikman.com/blog/2023/08/10/how-to-take-smart-notes/)
- Karpathy: raw sources are immutable, and the LLM only reads them. Wiki answers carry citations. `log.md` records every ingest. — [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)
- Meeting pipelines in practice:
  - Vellotti: Meetings/ (1on1s, standups, one-offs), with `/meeting-prep` pulling past notes and person profiles from Knowledge/. — [Aman Khan / Carl Vellotti](https://amankhan1.substack.com/p/how-carl-set-up-his-personal-os-in)
  - Karpathy lists team wikis fed by Slack and meetings as a use case. — [Karpathy gist](https://gist.github.com/karpathy/442a6bf555914893e9891c11519de94f)
- Imhoff's agent extracts entities (books, people, media) from free-form daily notes into structured resource notes with backlinks. This is capture-then-structure. — [Imhoff](https://stefanimhoff.de/agentic-note-taking-obsidian-claude-code)
- Privacy and provenance on the platform:
  - Auto memory is machine-local.
  - A `modified` timestamp is recorded.
  - External `@imports` in project files need approval, as protection against files others commit.
  - Memory files are excluded from transcript cleanup.
  - [Claude Code memory docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/memory)
  - Claude app: memory per project, incognito chats not saved, and admins can disable memory. — [Anthropic, Memory](https://www.anthropic.com/news/memory)
- Security: payloads planted in persistent memory files affect future sessions, and agents that write memory more aggressively are more exploitable (preprints, search snippets). — [arXiv 2609.13889](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13889); [arXiv 2606.04329](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.04329)

### Inferences

- MordomoOS's journal sections (Today, Decisions, Open loops, Runs) fit the "capture decisions and open loops, not raw data" guidance well.
- Requirements for provenance and safety:
  - Each consolidated MEMORY.md entry carries a source pointer (journal date and section, or file path), an author tag (user or agent), and a timestamp.
  - Agent-ingested content from untrusted sources (web, email, connectors) never lands directly in Facts or Decisions. It goes through the journal and needs user confirmation. This matches the poisoning research and Karpathy's supervised ingest.
- Privacy requirements:
  - per-area or per-folder exclusion from compilation into AGENTS.md (AGENTS.md may be committed or shared);
  - a "private" flag or a CLAUDE.local.md-style personal layer;
  - an incognito or no-memory mode for a session.
  - Indexed user folders could leak sensitive content into compiled maps, so compilation should include only pointers by default.
- MordomoOS has no meeting-notes pipeline. A common practitioner pattern is a meetings folder plus a person-profile file and a prep skill. It may be in scope as a capture source that feeds the journal's Decisions and Open loops.

### Gaps

- I found no primary practitioner write-up of an end-to-end meeting-transcript-to-memory pipeline with provenance (for example Granola or Otter into a vault) in this pass.
- I found no source on PII redaction practices for local AI second brains.
